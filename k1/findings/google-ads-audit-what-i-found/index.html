Google Ads audit: what I found when I read 200 real sites

DMDébora Malqui, Medido (marketingmedido.com)Reviewed Nov 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

A Google Ads audit usually looks inside the ad account. Many problems are on the website, where the conversions are counted. When I read 200 real sites, about three in ten showed no analytics tool at all. Some counted one visit twice. Some still ran Universal Analytics, which Google turned off. Check the site, not only the account.

Why I looked at the website

When you pay for Google Ads, the account tells you how many conversions you got. But the account only knows what your website sends to it. If the site sends the wrong signal, or no signal, the report in the account is wrong. Nobody sees it, because the account looks fine.

So I read the home page of 200 real websites and wrote down what I could see. I did this in two groups of 100 sites, on October 30 and November 1, 2026. The sites were not clients. I chose them from public lists of websites and added local businesses. They came from the United States, Latin America, Europe, Israel and other places.

I do not name the sites. I only report what I saw in total.

In the first group, 29 of 95 sites showed none. In the second group, it was 28 of 97. For these sites, I saw no tool that counts visits or actions.

There is a limit here. Some big companies measure with their own systems, and I do not recognize those. So "I saw no tool" does not always mean "they measure nothing". It does mean that a person who wants to check the numbers has nothing visible to check.

In the second group of 100 sites, two sent the same page view two or more times on a single page load. One was a Google Analytics 4 tag that sent five page views. The other was a Meta pixel that sent two. I confirmed both.

Why does this matter for Google Ads? If your site counts the same visit twice, any number built on it looks bigger than the real one. A lead form that counts twice makes each lead look cheaper than it is.

In the second group, I read 19 local businesses. Eight had no analytics tool. Seven had Google Ads. Three had a Meta pixel. For the seven with Google Ads, I could not see from outside if the tag counted the right things.

05verified

Some Meta pixels were set up wrongly.

Two kinds of problems were real. In one, the pixel loaded but sent nothing to Meta. In the other, the pixel sent events with no pixel number, so Meta cannot link them to the account. This is about Meta, not Google, but it shows the same thing: a tag can be on the page and still not work.

What this means for your ads

I cannot see your ad account. I also did not read how your ads perform. What I can say from these reads is this:

  • A tag on the page does not mean the count is right.
  • Counts that are too high are as common a risk as counts that are too low.
  • A missing tool is a risk you can see in two minutes. A double count is easy to miss.

The sample is small. It is not a study of all businesses, and I do not claim that the shares apply to yours. The point is the kind of mistake, not the exact share.

Five checks you can do today

  1. Read the status of each conversion action.In Google Ads, open Goals, then Summary. Each action has a status: Active, Needs attention, Misconfigured, or Awaiting conversions. Google explains each one in its help page. Ask why if the status is not Active.
  2. Compare the numbers with real life.Take the last 30 days. Count the conversions in the account. Then count the inquiries, calls and orders you really got. If the account shows 40 and you can find 12, ask why.
  3. Test one action yourself.Fill in your own form. Then check that the action is counted once. In Google Analytics 4, DebugView shows events as they arrive. It needs debug mode to be on.
  4. Ask which tools load on your site.Ask the person who built the site for a list. Look for old tools, like Universal Analytics, and for the same tool twice.
  5. Write the date and what you found.Next time, you can compare.

Google changes its menus often. These names follow Google's help pages as I checked them on November 19, 2026. If a name is different in your account, use the help page.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Google Ads audit?
It is a review of how your Google Ads account is set up and how results are counted. A good audit also checks the website, because the website sends the conversion signals that the account uses.
Can I do a Google Ads audit myself?
Yes, for the first checks. You can read the conversion status, compare the count with your real inquiries, and test one action. Changes to the tags on the site usually need the person who manages the site.
Why would my Google Ads conversions be wrong?
Common reasons are an action that counts something that is not a real contact, the same event counted twice, or a tag that no longer fires after a site change. The first step is to compare the number with real inquiries.
