Google Ads audit template: what a useful one must include

DMDébora Malqui, Medido (marketingmedido.com)Reviewed Nov 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

A useful Google Ads audit template has one row for each thing that can make your results wrong: who owns the account, what each conversion counts, which searches trigger your ads, where the ads show, and what a real customer costs. Each row needs three fields: what you see, what you expected, and what you will do. Copy the template below and fill it in with the account open.

Why most templates do not help

Many templates are long lists of yes-or-no boxes: "Are ad extensions used? Are keywords grouped?" You can tick all of them and still not know if your results are true.

The most common problem I see is not a missing setting. It is a number that nobody compared with real life. I read 200 real websites in two groups of 100 on October 30 and November 1, 2026. About three in ten showed no analytics tool at all. Four counted a visit more than once. Eighteen still sent data to Universal Analytics, which Google turned off. The Google Ads account looked fine from the inside in all of these cases.

So this template starts with the numbers, not with the settings.

The template

Copy this into a spreadsheet. Fill in one row per line. Add the date on top.

| # | Area | Where to look | What you see | What you expected | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ownership | Account access settings | Who is administrator? Is the account under your business? | You, as administrator | |
| 2 | Conversion status | Goals, then Summary | The status of each conversion action | Active | |
| 3 | What each conversion counts | Each conversion action's settings | A list: form, call, purchase, page view | Only real contacts | |
| 4 | Account vs. real life | Last 30 days | Conversions in the account / real inquiries you received | About the same | |
| 5 | Search terms | Campaigns, then Search terms (Insights and reports) | Terms that do not fit your business | Few or none | |
| 6 | Negative keywords | Campaign, list and account level | Are the bad terms from row 5 already excluded? Is any list not attached to a campaign? | Yes | |
| 7 | Locations | Campaign settings, Locations, Location options | Places targeted. Presence, or presence or interest | Matches where you serve | |
| 8 | Campaign settings | Each campaign's settings | Networks, languages, schedule, budget, dates | Matches what you think you pay for | |
| 9 | Cost of a real inquiry | Spend in 30 days / real inquiries | One number | Close to the cost per conversion in the account | |
| 10 | Website tools | Ask who manages the site | List of tools that load. Old ones? Duplicates? | One of each, no old ones | |

How to fill each row

Rows 1 to 3 are about trust. If you are not the administrator, or the account sits under someone else's business, write that down first. For row 2, Google's help page lists the statuses: Active, Needs attention, Misconfigured, and Awaiting conversions. For row 3, ask of each action: does this happen only when a real customer reaches out?

Row 4 is the center of the template. Count the conversions the account reports for the last 30 days. Then count the inquiries, calls, messages and orders you really received, from any source. If the account says 40 and you can find 12, the template has done its job. You have a question for whoever manages the account.

Rows 5 to 8 are about waste. The search terms report shows the searches that triggered your ads. Read every row you can. Google's help explains that negative keywords exclude terms, that they exist at campaign, list and account level, and that a list only works once it is attached to a campaign. For locations, Google's default also reaches people who show interest in your location, not only people who are there. If you serve one area, choose the option on purpose.

Rows 9 and 10 are about the cost and the source. Divide what you spent in 30 days by the real inquiries from row 4. As arithmetic: spend of 1,000 and 10 real inquiries is 100 for each. Compare it with the cost per conversion that the account shows. Then ask for the list of tools on your site. Google Ads only knows what the site sends it.

If a row does not add up

  • Row 2 is not Active. Open Google's troubleshooting page for conversion tracking status. Most causes are on the website: a tag that moved or a form that was rebuilt.
  • Row 4 shows more in the account. Send your own form once and see how many conversions appear. Two means a double count.
  • Row 4 shows less in the account. Some contacts do not pass through the form. List where your inquiries come from before you decide that the ads do not work.
  • Row 10 shows old tools. Ask the person who manages the site to remove them and keep one of each. Check the numbers again a week later.

Where this template stops

A template shows whether the counting is right. It does not tell you if the ads are a good idea, or how many customers came back. It is also a snapshot: a tag can break next week, so repeat the template after any change on the site.

Google changes its menus often. These names follow Google's help pages as checked on November 19, 2026. If a name differs in your account, use the help page.

What to do next

Frequently asked questions

What should a Google Ads audit template include?
It should include who owns the account, the status and definition of each conversion, a comparison with real inquiries, the search terms report, negative keywords, location settings, campaign settings, and the cost of a real inquiry.
How often should I use an audit template?
Use it once now, and again after any change to the website, the campaigns or the person who manages them. If nothing changes, a regular date such as once a quarter is a reasonable habit. There is no rule that fits every account.
Can I use the template without access to the account?
Rows 1 to 8 need the account. You can ask the person who manages it for screenshots of each screen. Row 10 does not need account access.
