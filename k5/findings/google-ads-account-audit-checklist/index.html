Google Ads account audit checklist: ten checks in one hour

DMDébora Malqui, Medido (marketingmedido.com)Reviewed Nov 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

Audit a Google Ads account in this order: who owns it, whether conversion tracking records, what each conversion counts, how the account compares with your real inquiries, which searches trigger your ads, what is excluded, where the ads show, the campaign settings, the cost of a real inquiry, and the tools on your website. Ten checks, about an hour, and a date on your notes.

Before you start

Have two things ready: access to the account, and a list of the inquiries, calls and orders you really received in the last 30 days. Without that list, you can check settings but not results.

The ten checks

1. Who owns the account, and who has access. Open the account's access settings and read the list of users. Your email should be an administrator. The account should sit under a business you control, not under an agency's own account.

2. The status of each conversion action. In Google Ads, open Goals, then Summary. Google's troubleshooting page describes the statuses. Active means the tag records as expected. Needs attention means there are action items. Misconfigured means conversions stopped because of a setup error. Awaiting conversions means none were recorded in the last 7 days.

3. What each conversion action counts. Write down every action: a form sent, a call, a purchase, a page view. Ask of each one: does it happen only when a real customer reaches out? A thank-you page that anyone can open will inflate the count.

4. The account against real life. Count the conversions in the account for 30 days. Count the real inquiries from your list. If the account says 40 and you can find 12, something counts twice or counts the wrong thing.

5. The search terms report. Select Campaigns, then Search terms, under Insights and reports. Mark every term that a customer of yours would never type: a job search, a free version, another city, another product.

6. The negative keywords. They exist at campaign level, in lists, and at account level. A list only works once it is attached to a campaign. Check that the bad terms from check 5 are excluded.

7. Where the ads show. In each campaign's settings, open Locations, then Location options. Google's default includes people who show interest in your location, not only people who are there. Choose the option on purpose.

8. The campaign settings. Networks, languages, schedule, daily budget, start and end dates. Compare each with what you believe you pay for. Old settings stay for years.

9. The cost of a real inquiry. Divide the spend in 30 days by the real inquiries. As arithmetic: 1,000 spent and 10 real inquiries is 100 each. Compare it with the cost per conversion in the account. A large gap is the most useful finding of the audit.

10. The tools on your website. Google Ads only knows what your site sends it. Ask the person who manages the site for a list of the tools that load. Look for old ones and for the same tool twice.

What I saw on real sites

I read the home page of 200 real websites in two groups of 100, on October 30 and November 1, 2026. They were not clients, and I do not name them. Check 10 is where the surprises were: about three in ten sites showed no analytics tool, four counted a visit more than once, and eighteen still sent data to Universal Analytics, which Google turned off.

This is a small sample. It is not a study of all businesses. It shows the kind of mistake, not how common it is on your site.

If a check fails

  • Check 2 is not Active. Most causes are on the website: a tag that moved, a page that changed, a form that was rebuilt.
  • Check 4 shows more in the account. Send your own form once and see how many conversions appear. Two means a double count.
  • Check 4 shows less in the account. Some contacts do not pass through a form. List where your inquiries come from before you decide that the ads fail.
  • Check 1 fails. If you cannot get in, that is a finding. Ask for read-only access.

Where this checklist stops

It shows whether the counting is right, not whether the ads are a good idea. It is a snapshot, so repeat it after any change to the site, the campaigns, or the person who manages them. Write the date and one line for each finding, so the next time you can compare.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Google Ads account audit?
It is a review of how an account is set up and how its results are counted. A useful one also checks the website, because the website sends the conversion signals that the account uses.
How often should I audit my Google Ads account?
Audit it whenever something changes: a new site, a new agency, a new form, a new tag. Between changes, pick a regular date such as once a quarter. There is no rule that fits every account.
What is the first thing to check in a Google Ads account?
Conversion tracking: whether each action records, and whether it counts a real customer contact. Every other number depends on it.
