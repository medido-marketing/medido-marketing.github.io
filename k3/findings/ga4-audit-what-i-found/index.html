GA4 audit: what I found when I read 200 real websites

DMDébora MalquiReviewed Nov 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer

A GA4 audit asks one thing: can you trust the numbers? I read 200 real websites. A little more than half sent data to Google Analytics 4. Four of them counted the same page view two or more times. Eighteen still sent data to Universal Analytics, which Google turned off. Having GA4 on the site does not mean the count is right. You can test it yourself in an hour.

What I looked at

I read the home page of 200 real websites in two groups of 100, on October 30 and November 1, 2026. They were not clients. I took them from public lists of websites and added small local businesses. They came from the United States, Latin America, Europe, Israel and other places.

I could read 192 of the 200. The other eight did not open or blocked the reading. I do not name any site. I only give totals.

What I found

01

About half the sites had GA4.

In the first group, 51 of 95 sites sent data to Google Analytics 4. In the second group, it was 54 of 97. That is 105 of 192.

The other sites are a mix. Some use other tools. Some big companies have their own systems. And about three in ten showed no analytics tool that I know.

02

Four sites counted one page view more than once.

I confirmed a double count on four sites, two in each group. On one of them, GA4 sent five page views for one page load. On another, a Meta pixel sent the same visit twice.

Four of 192 sounds small. But think about what it does. If every visit counts twice, your traffic looks twice as big, and every rate built on it is wrong. The report still looks clean. Nobody notices until someone tests it.

On 80 sites, I could confirm there was no double count. On the rest, I could not confirm it either way.

03

Eighteen sites still sent data to Universal Analytics.

Universal Analytics is the old version. Google turned it off. I still found it sending data on 13 sites in the first group and 5 in the second. Some were small local businesses.

This old code does nothing useful now. For me, it is a sign: nobody has looked at the measurement of that site for a long time. If the old tag is still there, I would also check that the move to GA4 was finished.

04

Some sites had more than one GA4 number.

Among the sites with GA4, 14 in the first group and 9 in the second sent data to more than one GA4 account number. This can be correct. A company can have a reason for two. But in a small business, it is often an old account that an earlier agency left behind. It is worth one question: who owns each number?

05

Small local businesses had less.

I read 39 small local businesses. Eighteen showed no analytics tool. Only 17 had GA4. These are businesses like a dentist, a plumber, a bakery or a law office. Many of them pay for ads.

What this means

I cannot see inside a GA4 account from outside. So I cannot say if the reports of these sites are wrong. What I can say is this:

  • GA4 on the page is the start, not the proof.
  • Old tags and extra account numbers are common, and nobody removes them.
  • A double count is rare but serious, and it is invisible in the report.

This is a small sample. It is not a study of all businesses. Do not use these shares as a rule for your own site.

Six checks you can do yourself

  1. See yourself arrive.Open the Realtime report in GA4. Visit your site in another tab. You should appear. Google describes this in its page "Confirm that you're collecting data".
  2. Count the page views of one visit.In GA4, go to Admin, then DebugView, under Data display. It shows events from one device as they arrive. It needs debug mode to be on. Load one page. If page_view appears twice, it is counted twice. Google notes that events may not show there if a visitor did not accept analytics cookies.
  3. Test your form and your contact button.Send your own form once. Check that it makes one event, not two and not zero.
  4. Read your key events.Key events are the actions you tell GA4 are important. In Admin, open Key events. For each one, ask: does this only happen when a real customer contacts me? Google says a change applies from that day on. It does not change old data.
  5. Remove your own visits.On a small site, your own team can be a big part of the traffic. GA4 lets you mark your IP addresses as internal traffic and filter them out. Google explains that the filter works only from the day you create it.
  6. Compare with real life.Take the last 30 days. Count the key events in GA4. Count the inquiries and orders you really got. A big gap, up or down, is a finding.

Google changes its menus often. These names follow Google's help pages as I checked them on November 19, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

What is a GA4 audit?
It is a review of your Google Analytics 4 setup. It checks that data arrives, that each action is counted once, and that the events marked as important are real customer actions.
How do I know if GA4 counts twice?
Open DebugView in GA4 with debug mode on and load one page of your site. One page load should make one page_view event. If you see two or more, the page view is counted more than once.
Do I still need to remove Universal Analytics code?
Universal Analytics no longer works, so the old code gives you nothing. Removing it is good cleaning. More important is to check that everything it measured is now measured in GA4.
