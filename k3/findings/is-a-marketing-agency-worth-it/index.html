Is hiring a marketing agency worth it? How to check what it reports

DMDébora MalquiReviewed Nov 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

A marketing agency is worth it when you can connect what you pay to real inquiries and sales, with numbers you can see yourself. Often nobody checks those numbers. When I read 200 real websites, I found old tags still running, visits counted twice, and small businesses with ads but no analytics tool. Ask your agency five questions, and check the answers yourself.

The real question

When owners ask "is it worth it?", they usually mean: "Is the report true?" The agency sends a report each month. The numbers go up. But the phone does not ring more.

Most agencies work honestly. The problem is different. An agency reports on its own work, and the report depends on how the website counts. If the count is wrong, the agency may not know it either.

Medido does not manage ads or accounts. So it wins nothing if you keep your agency, change it or stop.

What I saw on 200 real websites

I read the home page of 200 real websites on October 30 and November 1, 2026. They were not clients. I chose them from public lists and added small local businesses. I could read 192. I do not name any site.

I do not know which of these sites work with an agency. So these findings do not say that agencies are bad. They show what stays wrong when nobody checks.

1. Old tags that nobody removed. Eighteen sites still sent data to Universal Analytics, the old version that Google turned off. If someone looked at the measurement of those sites, this tag would be gone.

2. Visits counted twice. On four sites, I confirmed that one visit was counted two or more times. On one, a single page load sent five page views. A report built on that count looks better than the truth.

3. Ads with nothing to measure them. I read 39 small local businesses. Eighteen showed no analytics tool. In the second group of 19, seven had a Google Ads tag and eight had no analytics tool.

4. A pixel that sends nothing useful. On two sites, the Meta pixel loaded but sent nothing. On one, it sent visits with no pixel number, so Meta could not link them to the account.

5. A domain close to its end date. One site had a domain with an end date three weeks after I read it. If nobody renews a domain, the site and the email stop working. It is a small thing that someone must own.

Five signs that nobody is checking

  1. The report says "leads" or "conversions" but does not say what counts as one.
  2. The numbers in the report are much higher than the calls and messages you remember.
  3. You see clicks and impressions, but no cost for each real inquiry.
  4. You cannot open your own analytics or ad accounts.
  5. The accounts or the domain are in the agency's name, not in yours.

One sign is not proof of a problem. It is a reason to ask.

Five questions to ask your agency

Send them in writing. A good agency can answer in a day or two.

  1. Who manages our accounts, and can I have read-only access?The accounts should be yours, and you should be able to look.
  2. What counts as a conversion? Please list each one.A sent form and a button click are different things.
  3. Can any event be counted twice? How did you test it?A good answer describes a test.
  4. What did each real inquiry cost last quarter, by channel? What do you call "real"?"Real" should not include spam or wrong numbers.
  5. Who owns the domain and the ad accounts? What happens if we stop working together?The answer should be: you own them.

How to check the answers

  • Count for one month. Write down every real inquiry and order. Compare the total with the report.
  • Look yourself. With read-only access, open the conversion list in Google Ads and the key events in Google Analytics 4.
  • Ask for the months before. Ask for the same report for the three months before the agency started.
  • Give it time. Some channels need weeks. Use the time plan that the agency gave you.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth hiring a marketing agency for a small business?
It can be, if you cannot run the ads yourself and you can connect the results to real inquiries or sales. It is hard to defend when the ad budget is small next to the agency fee, or when nobody can check the results. There is no one number that fits every business.
How do I know if my agency's report is true?
Compare one month of the report with the inquiries and orders you really got. Ask for read-only access to your accounts. Ask how they tested for double counting. If the three do not agree, ask for an explanation.
Can I ask my agency for access to my accounts?
Yes. The accounts that measure and pay for your marketing should be yours. Read-only access is a normal thing to ask for.
